Amazon, Uber, and Apple ask different things at the SDE-2 level even when the underlying DSA overlaps — this page exists to show you exactly where they diverge. Each card breaks down pattern frequency, round structure, and what candidates actually reported in 2025–26 loops, so your prep time goes toward what a specific company rewards instead of a generic "top 150" list.

OA + Loop format: Online assessment is 2 DSA problems + LP survey (HackerRank). The onsite Loop runs 3–4 rounds and is roughly a 50/50 split between technical problems and Leadership Principle probes — each interviewer is typically assigned 2–3 specific LPs to dig into. 2026 reports lean heavily on Sliding Window (sum/max-in-window variants), Merge Intervals, and Dijkstra-flavored graph problems. Most rounds use a plain editor with no autocomplete or compiler, so practice dry-running examples by hand. Prep 6–8 STAR stories that map to Customer Obsession, Ownership, and Bias for Action.

Machine coding round: Uber is unusual in that both the DSA round and the machine-coding round require code that actually compiles and runs, not pseudocode on a whiteboard — interviewers (sometimes Staff-level) fire rapid follow-ups on edge cases and trade-offs. Recent 2026 reports include a 60-minute "design an in-memory Twitter" build. Prepare: Rate Limiter, Trip State Machine, Job Scheduler, Concurrent HashMap with getRandom. Segment trees show up at the hard end for SDE-2/L4.

Unique to Apple: ICT3 (~3–6 yrs) and ICT4 (~6–10 yrs) loops both fold Java + Spring Boot questions into the same 60-minute slot as the DSA problem — a confirmed 2025–26 pattern is 30 min on a problem like Trapping Rain Water followed by 30 min on Spring Cloud, Comparator vs Comparable, or default interface methods. CAP theorem, Factory pattern, and circuit breaking all come up. The single 45-minute system-design round at ICT3/4 expects a coherent end-to-end design, not exhaustive depth.
